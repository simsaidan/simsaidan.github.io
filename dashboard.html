A Logistic Regression-Based Tennis Match Rating System

This blog post is based on a work I did in Spring 2023 as an analyst with the Cornell Sports Analytics Club.

Introduction

The goal of this project was to come up with a way to rate tennis performances in a single match, looking at important match statistics, and assigning each player a number between 1 and 10. I utilized a logistic regression model to estimate the probability that a player wins a match based on 32 match statistics, which is then scaled to produce the rating. This approach accomplishes 2 goals:

  1. Provides a quantitative measure of player performance.
  2. Reveals which match statistics are most important in determining the outcome of a match.

The approach looks effective through preliminary testing, although more refinement and testing is needed. Overall, I think it's a novel and useful way of measuring tennis performances.

Motivation

Let's start by defining the difference between rating a player's ability and rating someone's performance. To illustrate these differences further, consider a scenario where Carlos Alcaraz plays a match on the ATP tour and a fifteen-year old plays a match in a local tournament on the same day. It is perfectly feasible that Carlos Alcaraz's performance out of 10 is worse than the junior player's, especially if Carlos makes many unforced errors that day and the fifteen-year old plays consistently. However, that fifteen-year old will almost certainly never have the tennis ability of Carlos Alcaraz. Currently, there are a wide variety of tools to rate the ability of a tennis player. The most popular and effective way is the Universal Tennis Rating , in which competitive tennis players are assigned a number between 1 (novice) and 16 (top professional men's player). There is also NTRP, an outdated system which rates player's abilities from 1 to 7, and a newer system called World Tennis Number Although there is a lack of a good performance rating system in tennis, that does not mean that one would not be valuable.
