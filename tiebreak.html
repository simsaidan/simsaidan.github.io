Background

Under most standard tennis scoring formats, tiebreakers are used to decide sets that are tied after a fixed number of games (7-point tiebreaker) or to decide matches that are tied one to one in sets (10-point tiebreaker). In both cases, the first player to that number of points wins but they must be ahead by two points or the tiebreaker continues. In addition, both tiebreaker formats use the “A-B-B-A” serving format in which the player/team who serves the first point also serves the fourth, fifth, eighth, ninth, and so on.

We now lay out important trends, terms, and rules in a standard tennis match. At almost every level of tennis, the player/team who is serving has an advantage. A hold is defined as the server winning the point or game. Likewise, a break is defined as the returner winning the point or game. When a returner is within one point of winning a game or tiebreaker, the next point is said to be a “break point.” Likewise, when either player is within one point of winning a match, the next point is said to be a “match point.” match point dynamic
